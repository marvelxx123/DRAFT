## 1. Brand Voice & Tone Guide

### The Three Words

**Warm. Sharp. Dependable.**

- **Warm** — CALLA sounds like a trusted colleague, not a software manual. It uses contractions, plain language, and acknowledges the human reality of running a small business.
- **Sharp** — Every sentence earns its place. No filler, no jargon, no circular phrasing. CALLA gets to the point because small business owners don't have time to wade through fluff.
- **Dependable** — Tone is consistent whether something's going right or wrong. CALLA doesn't over-celebrate or panic. It's steady, and that steadiness is the whole product promise.

---

### CALLA Always Says / Never Says

**CALLA Always Says:**
1. Concrete outcomes over abstract features. ("You haven't missed a call in 7 days" instead of "our AI is working hard for you.")
2. "Your business" — possessive, specific, personal. The business belongs to the owner and CALLA is in service to it.
3. Plain past/present tense. ("A call came in at 2:14 PM. Maria left a message. We sent her a follow-up text." Not: "Our system has processed an inbound voice interaction.")

**CALLA Never Says:**
1. "Leverage" / "synergy" / "best-in-class" / "robust" — hollow corporate vocabulary that signals nothing.
2. "Don't worry!" — dismissive and slightly condescending. If something needs attention, say what it is.
3. Passive constructions that obscure who did what. ("Your caller was handled" is banned. "CALLA answered and sent a follow-up" is correct.)

### On-Brand vs. Off-Brand Copy — 5 Scenarios

---

#### Scenario 1: Website Headline

**OFF-BRAND:**
> "An AI-powered virtual receptionist solution that ensures no business communication opportunity is missed."

*Why it fails:* "Solution," "AI-powered," "communication opportunity" — every word is borrowed from a pitch deck. It says nothing a real person would say. It does not respect the reader's time.

**ON-BRAND:**
> "Your phone gets answered. Every time. Even when you're up to your elbows in work."

*Why it works:* Specific visual ("up to your elbows"), addresses the exact moment of the problem, and the product promise is in the first four words.

---

#### Scenario 2: Error Message

**OFF-BRAND:**
> "An error has occurred. Please contact support. Error code: 4082-B."

*Why it fails:* Cold, opaque, and dumps the burden on the user without telling them what happened or what to do next.

**ON-BRAND:**
> "Something went sideways on our end — your calls are still being answered, but we couldn't send the follow-up SMS just now. We're on it. You can also retry manually below."

*Why it works:* Leads with reassurance (calls are still covered), owns the problem, tells the user their next option. Tone is calm and human.

---

#### Scenario 3: Onboarding Email Subject Line & Opening

**OFF-BRAND:**
> Subject: Welcome to the CALLA Platform — Getting Started Resources Enclosed
>
> "Thank you for registering. Please review the attached onboarding documentation to configure your account settings."

*Why it fails:* "Platform," "resources enclosed," "onboarding documentation" — this reads like an enterprise software deployment email. The user just signed up for something that's supposed to feel personal.

**ON-BRAND:**
> Subject: Your phone is about to stop ringing off the hook (in the best way)
>
> "You just made the last call you'll need to worry about missing. Here's what happens next — it takes about four minutes."

*Why it works:* The subject is a specific, relatable image. The opening reframes the onboarding not as a task but as relief already happening. "Four minutes" is respectful of their time.

---

#### Scenario 4: Social Post

**OFF-BRAND:**
> "CALLA leverages cutting-edge AI to provide seamless call management for SMBs. Increase your ROI today. #AI #SmallBusiness #VoiceAI"

*Why it fails:* Every word is a cliché. "SMBs," "leverage," "seamless," "ROI" — this is software marketing autopilot. It would get scrolled past in 0.3 seconds.

**ON-BRAND:**
> "A customer called your salon at 6:47 PM on a Tuesday. You were finishing a balayage. She hung up after four rings.
>
> She booked somewhere else.
>
> That's not a technology problem. That's a Tuesday problem. CALLA fixes it.
>
> #MissedCallsMissedMoney #SalonOwner #SmallBusinessLife"

*Why it works:* Hyper-specific scenario. Empathetic framing ("That's a Tuesday problem"). Punchy resolution. The hashtags are specific enough to reach the right people.

---

#### Scenario 5: Cold Outreach

**OFF-BRAND:**
> "Hi [Name], I'm reaching out to introduce CALLA, an innovative AI receptionist solution that can help your business improve call handling efficiency. Would you have 15 minutes for a demo?"

*Why it fails:* "Innovative," "solution," "call handling efficiency" — none of this is specific to the recipient. Asking for 15 minutes before establishing value is presumptuous.

**ON-BRAND:**
> "Hi Sarah — quick one.
>
> Most salons in [City] miss 30–40% of calls that come in after 5 PM or during peak hours. CALLA answers them, texts the caller back in 60 seconds, and asks them to leave a Google review if things went well.
>
> No hold music. No voicemail graveyard. Want to see a 2-minute walkthrough?"

*Why it works:* Opens with a specific, verifiable claim. Names the exact thing CALLA does. Addresses a real pain point (after-hours + peak hours). "2-minute walkthrough" is far less threatening than "15-minute demo."

## 2. Logo Concept Description

### Overview

The CALLA logo is a **combination mark**: a bespoke icon paired with a clean wordmark. The icon and wordmark can be used together as the primary lockup, or the icon can stand alone as a compact mark for app icons, favicon, and profile images.

---

### The Icon

**Concept: The Lily Wave**

The icon fuses two forms into one fluid shape:

A stylized calla lily, viewed from a three-quarter angle, with its characteristic trumpet-shaped spathe — but the interior of the spathe is replaced by three concentric, softly curved lines that read simultaneously as a **sound wave / voice frequency** and as the **interior curve of the flower**. The pistil (the yellow stamen at the center of a real calla lily) becomes a small, bright gold dot — the "answer" moment, the point of connection, visually the size of a contact button.

The lily's stem curves gently to the left, grounding the mark and giving it a sense of natural, unhurried confidence. The overall silhouette is contained within an invisible vertical oval — it should feel like a single, unbroken gesture.

**What it should NOT look like:** A clipart flower. A headset icon with a flower stuck to it. Anything that looks like it was assembled from separate elements. The integration of lily and sound wave should feel inevitable, not forced.

---

### Color Usage in the Logo

**Primary Lockup (on white or cream backgrounds):**
- Icon: Deep emerald (#0D4F3C) for the lily/wave form
- Gold dot (pistil/answer point): Warm gold (#E8C547)
- Wordmark "CALLA": Deep emerald (#0D4F3C), Inter, weight 300 (light), tracked at +80 letter-spacing. All caps. The wide tracking echoes the open, unhurried quality of the brand.

**Reversed Lockup (on dark or emerald backgrounds):**
- Icon: Clean white (#FAFAF8) for the lily/wave form
- Gold dot: Warm gold (#E8C547) — unchanged, it pops on both dark and light
- Wordmark: Clean white (#FAFAF8)

**Single-Color Version (for embossing, engraving, black-and-white printing):**
- Full mark in flat black or flat white. The gold dot becomes the same color as the rest of the icon — no special treatment. The mark still reads cleanly because the form carries the identity without needing color.

---

### Wordmark Details

- **Font:** Inter Light (weight 300), tracked generously (+80 to +100 units)
- **Case:** All-caps: **CALLA**
- **Relationship to icon:** Icon sits to the left of the wordmark, vertically centered, with spacing equal to roughly one character-width
- **Do not:** Use the wordmark in any weight heavier than Regular (400) in the primary lockup. Bold weights are available for headlines and UI but not for the logo itself — the lightness is intentional and signals elegance, not weakness.

---

### Tagline Lockup

For use on hero sections, business cards, and launch materials only:

```
[icon]  CALLA
        Every call, perfectly answered.
```

The tagline sits flush-left below "CALLA," set in Inter Light Italic, at roughly 40% of the wordmark's cap height. Color: warm gold (#E8C547) on light backgrounds, white (#FAFAF8) on dark.
