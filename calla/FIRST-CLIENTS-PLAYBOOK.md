# CALLA — FIRST CLIENTS PLAYBOOK
### Get 3 paying clients this week. No ads. No agency. Just DMs and phone calls.

This is the manual. You do this by hand first. When you've closed 3 clients, you automate it. Not before.

---

## DAY 1 — MONDAY MORNING: Find 30 Prospects in 1 Hour

### Step-by-step: Google Maps prospecting

You need 30 hair salons. Here's exactly how to find them.

1. Open Google Maps in a browser (not mobile — you need to see the sidebar list).
2. Search: `hair salon [your city]` — use your actual city name, not a metro area.
3. In the left sidebar, you'll see a list of results. Scroll through them.
4. For each salon, click through and collect the following. Do not skip any field.
5. After the first 20, zoom into a different neighborhood and repeat. Cover at least 2–3 zip codes.
6. Avoid chains (Great Clips, Supercuts, Sport Clips). You want owner-operated salons with 1 location.

**How to spot an owner-operated salon:**
- Fewer than 50 reviews (chains have hundreds)
- The owner's name appears in reviews ("Maria was amazing")
- The "Owner" has responded to at least one review
- They have an Instagram link on their Google listing

### What information to collect

For each salon, record these 7 fields:

| Field | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | Google listing | For personalization |
| Phone number | Google listing | Backup outreach |
| Google review count | Google listing | Low count = pain signal |
| Average star rating | Google listing | 1-star recent reviews = pain signal |
| Instagram handle | Their website or Google listing | Your primary outreach channel |
| Last post date on Instagram | Check their IG | Inactive = less likely to respond |
| Notes | Your observation | Anything that helps personalize |

### How to prioritize who to contact first

Not all 30 are equal. Sort them by pain signals. Contact the ones with the most pain first.

**Tier 1 (contact first) — high pain signals:**
- 10–40 Google reviews total (they haven't figured out review generation)
- A 1-star or 2-star review in the last 90 days that mentions something like "no one answered" or "had to leave a voicemail"
- Their last Instagram post was more than 2 weeks ago (they're busy or overwhelmed)
- No "Questions & Answers" section filled out on Google (not on top of their listing)

**Tier 2 (contact second):**
- 40–100 reviews, decent rating, but Instagram is active (they care about growth — use that)
- Owner responds to reviews personally (they're engaged, which means they'll engage with your DM)

**Tier 3 (contact last):**
- Over 150 reviews with 4.8+ rating (they may have a system already)
- Part of a small chain (2–3 locations) — harder sale, longer decision cycle

**One specific tactic:** Search Google Maps for `hair salon [city]` and sort by rating lowest-first. Every salon sitting at 3.8–4.2 stars is in pain. They know their score is dragging them down. CALLA SHIELD is the answer to that problem.

### Google Sheet template structure

Create a sheet called `CALLA Prospects — Week 1` with these columns:

```
Column A: Business Name
Column B: Instagram Handle
Column C: Phone Number
Column D: Google Review Count
Column E: Google Star Rating
Column F: Recent 1-Star? (Yes/No)
Column G: Last IG Post Date
Column H: Tier (1/2/3)
Column I: DM Sent? (Yes/No + date)
Column J: Reply Received? (Yes/No)
Column K: Demo Booked? (Yes/No + date/time)
Column L: Trial Started? (Yes/No)
Column M: Paid? (Yes/No)
Column N: Notes
```

Color code: Red = Tier 1, Yellow = Tier 2, White = Tier 3. Sort by column H before you start outreach.

## DAY 1 — MONDAY AFTERNOON: Send 10 DMs

You're not pitching. You're opening a conversation. The goal of the DM is one thing: get a reply.

Send to your top 10 Tier 1 prospects. Go to their Instagram profile. Send a DM (not a comment). Use this template.

### The 3-Line DM

```
Hey [Name] — your balayage work is stunning, especially that transformation you posted last week.

Quick question — do you ever miss calls while you're in the middle of a color or blowout? We built something specifically for that.

No pitch, just curious if it's actually a problem for you.
```

**How to personalize line 1:**
- Scroll their last 6 posts. Find one specific thing to compliment. Be specific — "your balayage work" beats "your content."
- If they post client transformations, mention the transformation. If they post their space, mention how clean or bright it looks.
- Do not say "I love your feed" or "great content." That's what every spam account says.

**Personalizing for salons with low reviews:**
If the salon has under 25 reviews or has a recent bad review about calls/voicemail, you can use this variant for line 2:

```
I noticed you're sitting at [X] Google reviews — a lot of salons your size are losing clients just from missed calls and never getting asked to leave a review.
```

**What not to do:**
- Do not include a link in the first DM. Instagram buries messages with links.
- Do not say "I'm from CALLA" or name your product. That turns it into a pitch before they're curious.
- Do not write more than 3 lines. Longer = ignored.

### The 48-Hour Follow-Up DM

If no reply after 48 hours, send exactly this — once:

```
Hey [Name] — just checking back on this. Totally fine if timing's off.

We're doing a free 7-day trial for a handful of salons this month and wanted to offer it to you first.

Worth a quick chat?
```

Send this once. If no reply after that, move on. Do not send a third message. Persistence past two touches is spam.

## DAY 2–3: HANDLE REPLIES AND BOOK DEMOS

### How to qualify fast — 2 questions only

When someone replies with curiosity ("what is it?" or "tell me more"), do not send a wall of text. Ask two questions before you book anything.

**Question 1:**
```
How many calls do you think you miss in a typical week — rough guess?
```

This gets them to say the number out loud. Even saying "probably 5 or 6" anchors the pain. If they say "none, we're always available" — they're not a fit right now. Move on.

**Question 2:**
```
Do you currently have any way to follow up with someone who called but didn't leave a voicemail?
```

If the answer is no (it almost always is), that's your green light. Book the demo.

### How to book the demo call

Use Calendly (free plan works). Set up a 20-minute slot labeled "CALLA Quick Demo."

Offer three specific windows when you reply:

```
Great — I'd love to show you exactly what it does in 15 minutes. I have:
- Tuesday at 10am
- Tuesday at 2pm
- Wednesday at 11am

Which works best, or is there a better time?
```

Always offer 3 slots. Giving one slot forces a yes/no. Giving 3 forces a choice.

**Booking rules:**
- Demo calls should be video (Zoom or Google Meet). Not phone. You need them to see the product.
- Morning slots work best for salon owners — they're often between clients, not yet slammed.
- Confirm the booking 1 hour before via DM: "Hey, we're on for [time] — here's the link: [link]"

### If they ask "how much does it cost?" before the demo

This is a buying signal, not a roadblock. Do not answer with a price. Answer like this:

```
Plans start at $79/month, but honestly the right plan depends on your call volume — some salons need more, some need less. The demo takes 15 minutes and I can tell you exactly what would work for you. Worth it?
```

This does three things: it gives them a real number (no games), it reframes price as dependent on their situation, and it re-asks for the demo. Most will say yes.

If they still push for price without a demo, send the pricing page link and say:

```
Here's the breakdown: [link]. The Pro plan at $149 is what most salons end up on. But I'll be honest — if you're not sold after the demo, the pricing won't matter either way. Want to do the quick call?
```

## THE DEMO CALL SCRIPT (15 minutes)

Do this on video. Have your Calendly confirmation open. Have a notepad ready for the two things they say during minutes 2–5 — you'll use those words verbatim when you close.

### 0:00–2:00 — Build rapport, ask about their business

Open with warmth, not business.

```
"Hey [Name], great to finally connect. I saw your work on Instagram — how long have you been at [salon name]?"
```

Let them talk for 60–90 seconds. You want to know: how long they've been there, whether they own it or work there, and whether they're solo or have a team. Those details shape your pitch.

If they own it: everything you say after this is about their business, their revenue, their reputation.
If they're a stylist with a chair: shift to how this helps them personally, not the whole salon.

End this section by transitioning:

```
"Got it. So I want to make sure I'm actually useful to you in the next 15 minutes — can I ask you a couple of quick questions first?"
```

### 2:00–5:00 — Diagnose the pain

Ask these questions in this order. Do not skip ahead. Listen.

**Question 1:**
```
"When you're in the middle of a cut or a color and your phone rings — what actually happens?"
```

Let them answer. Most will say something like "I let it go to voicemail" or "my front desk handles it" (if they have one). If they say they always answer, ask: "What about when you're both busy at the same time?"

**Question 2:**
```
"Do you know how many calls you actually miss in a week? Have you ever looked at that?"
```

Most have no idea. That's fine. That's the point.

**Question 3:**
```
"If someone calls and hangs up without leaving a voicemail — what happens to that person?"
```

Pause here. Let the silence sit for a second. The answer is: nothing. That person is gone. They'll say it themselves, or you say it for them:

```
"Right — they just disappear. No way to follow up, no record they even called. And they might be booking at another salon right now."
```

Write down the exact words they use when they describe the problem. You'll use those words in the close.

**Question 4 (only if they have a Google listing):**
```
"How do you currently get new Google reviews? Do you ask clients, or does it just kind of happen?"
```

Almost no one has a system. Most say "I ask sometimes" or "clients just leave them." This sets up CALLA SHIELD.

### 5:00–8:00 — Show CALLA

You don't need a live demo. Describe the experience.

```
"Here's what CALLA looks like from your client's perspective. It's Monday at 2pm. You're elbow-deep in a balayage. Your phone rings. Instead of going to voicemail, CALLA answers in your salon's voice — customized to sound like your business. It says something like: 'Thanks for calling [Salon Name]. We're with a client right now — can I help you book an appointment or answer a quick question?'

The client says they want to book a cut for Saturday. CALLA walks them through available slots, books it, and sends them a confirmation text — all without you touching your phone.

The client hangs up happy. You find out about the booking when you're done with your current client. That's it."
```

Then pause and ask:

```
"How many times a week do you think that scenario plays out for you right now?"
```

Let them do the math. If they say 10 calls a week and they miss 5, that's 5 potential bookings lost. At $50–$100 per appointment, that's $250–$500 a week walking out the door.

### 8:00–10:00 — The CALLA SHIELD moment

This is where you slow down and make it feel different from everything else they've seen.

```
"Okay, so what I just described — that's the answering part. Every answering service does some version of that. Here's what CALLA does that nobody else does.

After every call — whether CALLA booked an appointment or just took a message — it automatically sends the caller a personalized text. Not a generic 'thanks for calling.' A message that references what they called about.

If someone called to reschedule, the text confirms the reschedule and tells them you're looking forward to seeing them. If someone called with a complaint or sounded frustrated, CALLA flags that for you immediately — you get a notification that says 'This caller may need personal follow-up.' You can call them back before they post a 1-star review.

And here's the part that gets salon owners every time: if someone called and the call went well — they booked, they sounded happy — CALLA sends them a text 2 hours later that says 'We loved chatting with you — if you have 30 seconds, we'd love a Google review: [direct link].' No asking awkwardly in person. No forgetting to follow up. Just reviews, automatically, from people who already like you."
```

Then pause and let it land.

If they had a 1-star review from a missed call — and you saw this before the call — now is when you bring it up:

```
"I actually noticed you have a review from [timeframe] that mentioned not getting a callback. CALLA would have caught that before it became a review."
```

Ask:

```
"How many Google reviews do you think you'd have right now if every happy caller had gotten that text?"
```

Let them answer. Then move to pricing.

### 10:00–12:00 — Pricing

Anchor high. Land on Pro.

```
"So pricing. There are three plans. The entry plan is $79/month — that's basic call answering and appointment booking. The Pro plan is $149/month — that's everything I just showed you, including CALLA SHIELD, the SMS follow-up, the review prompts, and the frustration alerts. The Premium plan is $249/month and adds multi-location support and priority escalation.

For a solo salon or small team, Pro is almost always the right fit. That's $149 a month. To put that in perspective: if CALLA recovers one missed appointment a week — one — at $75 a cut, that's $300 a month you were leaving on the table. CALLA pays for itself inside the first week."
```

Do not apologize for the price. Do not say "it's only." Just state it clearly and let the math land.

### 12:00–15:00 — Close + next steps

Don't ask "what do you think?" That invites vague objections. Instead, move to the trial.

```
"Here's what I want to do. I want to set you up on a 7-day free trial — no credit card required. We get CALLA answering your calls by tomorrow. At the end of 7 days, you'll have real data: how many calls came in, how many were booked, how many SHIELD texts went out, how many Google reviews you got. If the numbers make sense, you stay on. If not, you walk away and nothing changes. Fair?"
```

Then stop talking. Wait for a yes or an objection.

## THE CLOSE

### The trial offer script

Use this verbatim after your pricing explanation:

```
"Let me set you up for free for 7 days. No credit card, no commitment. I just need your business phone number and I can have CALLA live before you open tomorrow. You run it for a week — real calls, real clients — and at the end you tell me if it made your life easier. If it did, you decide whether it's worth $149 a month. If it didn't, we shake hands and that's it. Sound good?"
```

Key mechanics of this close:
- "No credit card" removes the financial risk objection before they can raise it.
- "Before you open tomorrow" creates urgency without a fake deadline.
- "You tell me" keeps the power with them. People close themselves when they feel in control.

### If they say "let me think about it"

Do not say "of course, take your time." That's a soft close on walking away. Say this instead:

```
"Totally fair. What's the part you want to think about? Is it the price, or is it more whether this would actually work for your specific setup?"
```

This forces specificity. A vague objection becomes a real one you can address. If it's price, revisit the math. If it's whether it works, offer the trial as the proof:

```
"That's exactly what the trial is for. You don't need to decide if it works today — CALLA shows you. Let me just get you set up and you'll know for sure after 7 days."
```

### If they say "send me info"

This almost always means no. But it's recoverable. Do not send a PDF and wait. Do this:

```
"Happy to send it over. Before I do — what's the specific thing you want to see? Like, is it the pricing breakdown, how the tech works, or something else? I want to send you what's actually useful."
```

If they can't name a specific thing, they're not interested yet. You've failed to establish enough pain or the SHIELD moment didn't land. Try this recovery:

```
"Honest question — was there something in the demo that didn't feel relevant to how you actually run things? I want to make sure I'm not wasting your time with something that doesn't fit."
```

This is a pattern interrupt. Most sales people push harder here. You're pulling back and asking for feedback. That builds trust and sometimes reopens the conversation.

If they still say "just send the info" — send one clean page with pricing and one line: "The 7-day free trial is open if you want to try before you decide — just reply here and I'll set it up." Then follow up in 3 days.

## DAY 5: FOLLOW UP AND CONVERT TRIALS TO PAID

Trials go cold without nurture. Here is the exact message sequence.

### Trial Day 1 (the day after they start)

Send via DM or text, whichever channel you used to close them:

```
"Hey [Name] — CALLA's been live for about 24 hours. How's it feeling from your end? Any calls come through yet?"
```

Keep it casual. You're checking in, not selling. If they say "yes, it answered a call" — that's a win to celebrate. If they say they haven't noticed anything — check the backend and report back: "Looks like you had 3 calls come in yesterday — I'll send you the summary."

### Trial Day 3

Send a mini data summary. Pull from their CALLA dashboard whatever is real:

```
"Quick update from your first 3 days on CALLA:
- [X] calls answered
- [X] appointments booked automatically
- [X] SHIELD texts sent to callers
- [X] Google review prompts sent

You're on pace for [X] reviews this month just from the automated follow-up. Wanted you to see the actual numbers."
```

Make the data feel real and specific. This is the most powerful close you have. Numbers from their own business beat any sales pitch.

### Trial Day 7 (close day)

This is the conversion message. Send it in the morning, before they get busy:

```
"Hey [Name] — your 7-day trial wraps up today. Here's what CALLA did for your business this week:

- Answered [X] calls while you were with clients
- Booked [X] appointments automatically
- Sent [X] SHIELD follow-up texts
- Generated [X] Google review prompts
- [X] new reviews posted to your listing

To keep it running, it's $149/month — less than 2 appointments. Want me to activate the account?"
```

If they had zero calls during the trial (unlikely, but possible), pivot:

```
"Looks like it was a quieter week on incoming calls — that happens. The best way to see CALLA work is during a busy stretch. Want to extend the trial another week during a busier period, or do you want to just go ahead and activate so it's ready when you need it?"
```

### Using CALLA SHIELD data to close

The single most powerful close you have is their own data. If CALLA caught a frustrated caller, say:

```
"CALLA flagged a frustrated caller on Day 2 — do you remember someone calling about [topic]? You got a heads-up before it became a review. That's SHIELD working. That one alert alone could have saved a 1-star review."
```

If they got new Google reviews during the trial:

```
"You got [X] new Google reviews this week from CALLA's automated prompts. At your current pace, that's [X × 4] reviews a month — you'd hit [Y total reviews] by the end of the year. That moves the needle on how you show up in local search."
```

People buy outcomes, not features. Show them the outcome that already happened.

## THE NUMBERS

Here is the math on 30 DMs, done honestly.

### Conversion funnel

| Stage | Count | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| DMs sent | 30 | 100% |
| Replies received | 10 | 33% |
| Demos booked | 4 | 40% of replies |
| Trials started | 2 | 50% of demos |
| Paid conversions | 1 | 50% of trials |

**Week 1 revenue from 30 DMs: $79–$149/month (1 client)**

That sounds small. Here's why the math compounds.

### The actual math

One client at Pro plan = $149/month.

Annual value of that one client (assuming 12-month retention): **$1,788/year**

If you convert at that rate from 30 DMs weekly:
- Week 1: 1 client = $149/month
- Week 2 (another 30 DMs): 1 more client = $298/month cumulative
- Week 3: $447/month cumulative
- Week 4: $596/month cumulative
- **Month 2, Week 1: You're at $745/month from 150 total DMs**

The goal for Week 1 is not to get rich. The goal is to prove the playbook works and to get your first 3 paying clients.

**To get 3 clients:**
- Send 90 DMs (30 per day × 3 days, or 30/day over the first week)
- Expect 27–30 replies
- Book 10–12 demos
- Start 5–6 trials
- Close 3 paid accounts

**3 clients at $149/month = $447/month recurring**

That's $5,364 in annual recurring revenue from one week of outreach, no ad spend, no agency, no tools except Instagram and Calendly.

### Why these numbers are conservative

The 33% reply rate assumes cold DMs with basic personalization. If you do the research and genuinely personalize line 1, reply rates in this niche run 40–50%. If your first SHIELD testimonials come in during Week 2 ("I got 4 new Google reviews in a week"), that lifts demo-to-trial and trial-to-paid materially. The model gets better as you get data.

## WEEK 2 ONWARD: SCALE WHAT WORKED

### The doubling rule

In Week 2, send 60 DMs. In Week 3, send 90. Do not skip steps.

The reason you don't jump to 100 DMs immediately: you need to refine the DM based on what's getting replies. After 30 sends, you'll know:
- Which compliment openers get the most responses (transformation posts vs. space photos vs. client work)
- Whether "do you miss calls" or "do you have a Google review system" lands better in line 2
- Which objection comes up most in DMs ("we have a receptionist," "we already use voicemail," "how much does it cost?")

Revise the template based on what's working before you increase volume.

### Metrics to track weekly

Every Friday, log these:
- DMs sent
- Reply rate (%)
- Demo book rate (of replies)
- Trial start rate (of demos)
- Trial-to-paid rate
- Churn (clients who cancel after first payment — this matters starting Month 2)

If your reply rate drops below 20%, your opener isn't working. Rewrite line 1.
If your demo-to-trial rate drops below 40%, the SHIELD moment isn't landing. Practice the script.
If your trial-to-paid rate drops below 40%, the Day 7 close message isn't hitting. Use more specific data.

### When to bring in other channels

After 10 paying clients, add one new channel per week — do not add all of these at once:

1. **Google Maps direct call** — call the salon phone during off-peak hours (Tuesday–Wednesday, 9–11am). Use the same opener as the DM, adapted for voice.
2. **Email** — once you have 10 clients and can write a case study ("Hair salon in [city] got 12 new Google reviews in 30 days"), email becomes viable. Before that, you don't have proof.
3. **Referrals** — after a client's first 30 days, ask: "Do you know 2 other salon owners I should talk to?" Happy clients refer freely if you ask at the right moment (right after they see their SHIELD data for the first time).

### When to stop doing this manually

You stop doing this manually when you hit $3,000/month in MRR (roughly 20 clients at Pro). At that point:

- You have enough testimonials for cold outreach to convert at higher rates with less effort
- You can afford to pay a VA $15–$20/hour to run the prospecting and DM steps (Steps 1–3 in this playbook)
- The demo and close still stay with you until you hit $10K MRR — those are the highest-leverage hours in the business

**The AI Outreach Agent takes over when you have:**
- A proven DM template (minimum 50-DM sample with known reply rate)
- A working Calendly flow that auto-confirms and reminds
- At least 5 trial data sets you can use as social proof in outreach
- A trained close script with documented objection handles

Before you have all four of those things, automation will just scale a broken process. Do it by hand. Get the reps. Then hand it off.
